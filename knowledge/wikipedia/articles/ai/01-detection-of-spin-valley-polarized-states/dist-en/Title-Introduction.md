Detection of Spin- and Valley-Polarized States — New Methods Without Need for Strong Magnetic Fields

Introduction

Paper: Detection of spin- and valley-polarized states
Authors: Olajunjoye A. Awoga et al. | arXiv: 2604.02427

In two-dimensional materials, electrons carry charge and spin — but they also possess a hidden degree of freedom called the "valley." This valley degree of freedom is directly tied to next-generation quantum computing. But detecting it has always required strong magnetic fields or precision optical equipment, creating extremely high barriers for experiments.

Researchers found two new methods: using the thermoelectric effect and current rectification — measuring temperature differences or changes in current flow direction — to directly read out valley polarization intensity. This means experimental physicists need no magnetic field at all, just an ordinary electrical measurement setup, to take a "photo" of valley polarization.
