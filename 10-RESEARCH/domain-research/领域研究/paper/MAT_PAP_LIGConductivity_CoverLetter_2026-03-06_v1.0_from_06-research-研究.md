We are pleased to submit our original research article entitled **"Machine Learning-Assisted Prediction of Electrical Conductivity in Laser-Induced Graphene Using Gaussian Process Regression"** for consideration for publication in *Carbon*.

## Background and Motivation

Laser-Induced Graphene (LIG) has emerged as a promising technique for direct-write graphene fabrication, offering advantages of low cost, rapid processing, and ambient operation. However, the electrical conductivity of LIG depends on multiple coupled process parameters (laser power, scanning speed, wavelength, etc.), making experimental optimization time-consuming and costly. Currently, there is no predictive tool available for estimating LIG conductivity from process parameters.

## Key Contributions

This work presents the first machine learning-based predictive model for LIG electrical conductivity. Our main contributions are:

1. **Largest LIG Dataset:** We collected and curated 200 data points from 15 literature sources, representing the largest publicly available LIG dataset.

2. **High-Accuracy Prediction:** Using Gaussian Process (GP) regression, we achieved R虏 = 0.801 on an independent test set, with mean absolute error of 459 S/m.

3. **Uncertainty Quantification:** The Bayesian nature of GP provides reliable confidence intervals, with 95% coverage rate of 100%.

4. **Feature Analysis:** We identified laser energy density as the most critical feature, with scanning speed showing negative correlation.

5. **Open Science:** All data, code, and pre-trained models will be publicly available at: https://github.com/shushuzn/lig-conductivity-prediction
